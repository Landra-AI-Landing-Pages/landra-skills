# Worked example: a sonic dermaplaning tool

Use this to see the level of detail expected. Don't copy the wording for a different product.

## The research (excerpt)

- "The way the foundation sits on my peach fuzz looks so bad to me." (r/makeuptips)
- "at least 95% of women I see don't have as much peach fuzz… as I do" (r/30PlusSkinCare)
- Laser "got rid of dark hairs only" (Trustpilot review)
- Tried waxing, depilatory creams, shaving (Trustpilot, Reddit)
- "my face has felt like sandpaper for weeks" (Reddit, after shaving)
- "Love how soft and smooth my skin feels" (Trustpilot review)
- "the charger stops working" (Amazon "Customers say")

## Stage 1: avatar snapshot (short)

AVATAR SNAPSHOT: "Sam", early 50s, removes peach fuzz from their face and worries it makes things worse

- The problem, in their words: "the way the foundation sits on my peach fuzz" (r/makeuptips)
- The moment it hits: putting on makeup. Foundation shows the fuzz.
- How it feels: like the only one ("at least 95% of women I see don't have as much...")
- What they think causes it: having more fuzz than other people
- What they tried: laser (only works on dark hair), waxing, creams, shaving
- What they want: "soft and smooth" skin, makeup that goes on smooth
- What scares them: rough regrowth ("sandpaper for weeks"). Also the tool breaking ("the charger stops working")
- Words they use: peach fuzz, foundation sits, soft, smooth, grow back, sandpaper
- Words they'd never use: vellus hair, exfoliation, radiant complexion
- Awareness: solution-aware. They know the fixes and have tried several.

Strongest evidence: makeup sitting on the fuzz, fear of rough regrowth, laser not working on light hair.
Gaps: no price objection data. No quotes on how often they'd need to repeat it. Find: 1 to 3 star reviews of competing tools.

## Stage 2: five angles

The obvious one (every brand runs it): "Smooth, glowing skin with dermaplaning." Named so we don't write five versions of it.

ANGLE 1: The sandpaper fear  (fear killer)
The angle: You can remove peach fuzz without the rough regrowth everyone warns you about.
Who it's for: people who've shaved their face once and hated how it felt after
Flips: "removing it makes it worse"
Promises: how to remove it and keep the skin smooth
Stands on: 3 quotes, e.g. "my face has felt like sandpaper for weeks"
Awareness: solution-aware   Best as: ad + advertorial
Example headline: How to remove peach fuzz without the rough, sandpaper regrowth
Watch out: any blade (a razor or a dermaplaning tool) cuts the hair, so it grows back with a blunt tip. Only promise "no rough regrowth" if the brand has real evidence for it. Without that, this angle can't run as written.

ANGLE 2: Laser missed it  (not your fault / tried everything)
The angle: If laser didn't work on your peach fuzz, it's not you. Laser works on dark hair, and peach fuzz is light.
Who it's for: people who paid for laser and still have fuzz
Flips: "I'm a hard case"
Promises: why it failed and what works on light hair
Stands on: 2 quotes, e.g. laser "got rid of dark hairs only"
Awareness: solution-aware   Best as: advertorial
Example headline: Laser didn't touch your peach fuzz? It's not you. It's the color of the hair.

ANGLE 3: The foundation tell  (outcome / how-to)
The angle: The reason your foundation looks cakey might be the fuzz under it, not the foundation.
Who it's for: people who've switched foundations and still see texture
Flips: "I need a better foundation"
Promises: makeup that goes on smooth
Stands on: 2 quotes, e.g. "the way the foundation sits on my peach fuzz"
Awareness: problem-aware   Best as: advertorial + ad
Example headline: It's not your foundation. It's the peach fuzz underneath it (and the 2-minute fix).
Watch out: "2-minute" only if the brand publishes it.

ANGLE 4: Peach fuzz over 50  (call-out)
The angle: Peach fuzz shows more as skin changes. Here's how people over 50 are handling it.
Who it's for: the 50+ slice of the research
Flips: "it's just me"
Promises: a fix that works on light, fine hair
Stands on: the age signal in the research (check the count before running this)
Awareness: problem-aware   Best as: listicle + ad
Example headline: How to get rid of peach fuzz on your face when you're over 50
Watch out: only run if the research really skews 50+. Use "people over 50", not a gender, unless the brand chooses to target one.

ANGLE 5: The thicker-hair myth  (myth breaker)
The angle: Removing peach fuzz doesn't make it grow back thicker. The rough feel comes from cutting the hair, not from more hair.
Who it's for: people avoiding it because of what they've heard
Flips: "if I remove it, it'll come back worse"
Promises: the truth, and how to avoid the rough feel
Stands on: the regrowth fear (3 quotes) + a well known fact
Awareness: problem-aware   Best as: ad (great hook) + advertorial
Example headline: Will peach fuzz grow back thicker if you remove it? Here's what actually happens.

My pick: Angle 3. The product clearly delivers it (the fuzz comes off, the makeup sits smoother), it stands on the most vivid quote in the research, and it surprises people who've blamed their foundation. Angle 1 has more quotes but needs proof the brand may not have (see its Watch out).

Note on a tempting angle: "Why you shouldn't shave your face over 50" sounds strong, but a dermaplaning tool IS a blade on the face. That angle argues against the product. The honest version is Angle 5.

## Stage 3: starts for Angle 3

### Advertorial start

Candidates:
1. (the obvious one) Dermaplaning: the secret to smooth, glowing skin
2. (mistake) The foundation swap that never fixes cakey makeup (and what does)
3. (who it's for) Why foundation starts catching on your skin after 50 (and the fix that isn't a new foundation)
4. (myth breaker) A better foundation won't hide peach fuzz. Here's what will.
5. (real cause) It's not your foundation. It's the peach fuzz underneath it (and how to get rid of it).

My pick: 5. It flips what they blame and the promise is visible.

Subheadline: Why makeup catches on the fine hair on your face, and the at-home fix people do before they put anything on.

Opening mode: the reader's past decision (they've already tried new foundations).

Opening:
> You've probably switched foundations more than once trying to get it to sit smooth.
>
> That made sense. When makeup looks cakey, the makeup is the obvious suspect.
>
> But for a lot of people, the problem isn't the formula. It's the fine, light hair underneath it. The peach fuzz catches the product, and it shows the second you're in good light.

### Listicle start (problem-led, 5 items)

Candidates:
1. (obvious) 5 reasons people are switching to dermaplaning
2. (benefit inside) 5 reasons people over 50 use dermaplaning for makeup that goes on smooth
3. (problem inside) 5 reasons your foundation still looks cakey after you've switched brands
4. (narrow group) 5 things to know about peach fuzz if laser didn't work for you
5. (correction) 5 things people get wrong about makeup and peach fuzz

Pick: 5. Subheadline: Number 1 is why switching foundations never fixed it.

Items:
1. A new foundation won't hide it
2. Laser can't see light hair
3. It won't grow back thicker
4. Waxing isn't the only way to remove it
5. You can do it at home in a few minutes

(Each item gets its proof in the body. Item 5 needs the brand's real time per session.)

### Ad start

Hooks:
1. "The way the foundation sits on my peach fuzz looks so bad to me." If that's you, it's not your foundation.
2. It's not your foundation. It's what's under it.
3. Switched foundations again and it still looks cakey?
4. If your foundation catches on your peach fuzz, watch this.
5. Before you buy another foundation, look at your face in the car mirror.

Script opening (from hook 1):

```
HOOK (0-3s)
VO: "'The way the foundation sits on my peach fuzz looks so bad to me.'"
On screen: the quote as a Reddit comment card, close-up of foundation on a cheek

RECOGNITION (3-8s)
VO: "So you buy a new foundation. Then another one. It still looks cakey in the car mirror."
On screen: three foundation bottles on a counter, then the car mirror

TURN (8-15s)
VO: "Because it was never the foundation. It's the fine hair underneath it, and it takes a few minutes to fix."
On screen: text "It's not your foundation."
```

Page this ad clicks through to: candidate 5 above ("It's not your foundation. It's the peach fuzz underneath it").
Check before running: "a few minutes" only if the brand publishes it.
