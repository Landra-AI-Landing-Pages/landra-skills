# Openings: the first lines of an advertorial, a listicle and an ad script

You write the start, not the whole thing. The start has one job: make the right reader read the next line.

---

## Advertorial opening (the hook)

**The one hard rule:** for the first 40% of an advertorial, it reads like an article. In the opening you write:
- no product name
- no button, link or "buy / shop / order / claim"
- no "Introducing..."

The product shows up later as a recommendation, once the reader cares about the problem.

### Pick one of five opening modes

**1. Orient the reader** (the safest default for cold traffic)
Start from what the reader already knows or is already asking.
- "If you're thinking about [kind of product], you've probably asked [the real question they ask]."
- "Most people who [do X] are weighing one trade-off: [the actual trade-off]."
Fails when it invents a scene the reader isn't in.

**2. Investigation**
Say up front that this is a comparison or a finding.
- "We compared [N] ways to [solve the problem]. Here's what we found."
Only if the comparison is real and the page will actually show it.

**3. Category observation**
A dry, specific fact about the category the reader may not have thought about.
- "Most [products in the category] are built for [X]. Almost none for [Y]."
Say it like reporting, not like an accusation.

**4. Counter-claim**
A direct line that reframes what they've been doing.
- "[Problem] isn't a [assumed cause] problem. It's a [real cause] problem."
- "Stop [the common fix]."
The next paragraph has to explain why, straight away.

**5. The reader's past decision**
Take a choice they already made and look at it again, on their side.
- "You tried [fix]. Maybe twice."
- "You switched to [X] because of [reason]. It didn't do what you hoped."
Never make them feel stupid for the choice. It was reasonable with what they knew.

### Rhythm
- Paragraphs of 1 to 3 sentences.
- Mix lengths: a one-line paragraph, then a three-line one, then one line.
- Mix sentence lengths inside a paragraph.
- Use at least one exact customer phrase in the first three paragraphs.

### The "how did they know?" test
Every line about their problem should make the reader think "how did they know that's exactly my life?" Use one or two of:
- **Physical:** a specific body part, texture or feeling ("feels like sandpaper").
- **Moment:** a specific situation ("in the car mirror", "in every group photo"). Not a clock time.
- **Object:** a specific thing in their life ("the foundation brush", "the leash by the door").
- **Their word:** their phrase, not the category's.

"You feel self-conscious" fails. "Your foundation catches on the fuzz along your jaw" passes.

### Don't
- Open with a clock time ("It's 2:47pm..."), "Picture this", "Imagine", "In today's world", "If you're like most people", "You've probably heard about...".
- Interview a founder ("We sat down with...").
- Use caps and exclamation marks, or call anything a miracle or a game-changer.

## Listicle opening

A listicle goes from the headline and subheadline straight into item 1. No long intro. If you write a lead-in, it's one or two sentences.

Write the item titles first. Each one is:
- a reason, written as a statement (not a question)
- something the reader can scan in two seconds
- built on a real quote or fact where possible
- different from the others (no two items arguing the same point)

Order: lead with the item that matches the headline's promise most directly. Put the item that answers the biggest fear in the top three. The product comes in near the end (problem-led) or from item 1 (product-led).

## Ad script opening (about the first 15 seconds)

People decide line by line whether to keep watching. Line 1 has to sell line 2. Line 2 has to sell line 3.

### Structure for the opening
1. **Hook (first 1 to 3 seconds).** One line that opens a question the viewer wants answered. Built from the angle and, ideally, their exact words.
2. **Recognition (next 3 to 5 seconds).** The moment or feeling only they know, so they think "that's me".
3. **The turn (next 5 to 7 seconds).** The belief flip, the real cause or the fear answered. This leads into the product, but you stop before the pitch.

Write it as a script with a visual note per line:

```
HOOK (0-3s)
VO / on camera: "[line]"
On screen: [text overlay / what we see]

RECOGNITION (3-8s)
VO: "[line]"
On screen: [...]

TURN (8-15s)
VO: "[line]"
On screen: [...]
```

### Hook shapes that work for ads
- **Call-out:** "If you're over 50 and your makeup sits on your peach fuzz, watch this."
- **Their quote:** say the customer's own line as the first words. "'My face felt like sandpaper for weeks.'"
- **Myth:** "Shaving your face does not make the hair grow back thicker. Here's why it feels like it does."
- **Not your fault:** "It's not your foundation. It's what's under it."
- **Fear killer:** "How to get rid of peach fuzz without the rough regrowth."
- **Tried everything:** "Tried waxing, creams and laser and it's still there?"

### Rules for ad openings
- Specific beats vague. "Sandpaper" beats "rough". A real number beats "a lot".
- One idea per line. Short lines.
- Don't open with "Hey guys", the brand name, or a question with an obvious answer ("Do you want better skin?").
- Every loop you open must pay off. Never tease something the ad doesn't deliver.
- Same truth rules as the page: no invented results, reviews or numbers.
- Message match: the page headline should feel like the next line after the ad.
