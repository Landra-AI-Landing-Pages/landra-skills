# Avatar checklist: what to look for

An avatar is useful only when it gives you things to write with. Go through the avatar AND the research behind it and pull out each of the fields below. Each field says what it's for, so you know why you're looking.

Quote exactly. Keep the source next to every quote (subreddit, review site, support ticket, link). Count how many quotes back each point.

## The 12 fields

**1. The problem, in their words**
How they describe it when nobody is selling to them. Not the category name.
Used for: headlines, hooks, the first line of the page.
Example: "the way the foundation sits on my peach fuzz" (not "facial hair removal").

**2. The moment it hits**
When and where they notice the problem: getting ready, a photo, a meeting, a date, a mirror in bad light.
Used for: opening scenes, ad script openings, the "how did they know?" feeling.

**3. How it makes them feel**
Embarrassed, alone, annoyed, tired of it, worried it's getting worse.
Used for: the emotional half of a hook. Look for words like "only one", "hate", "embarrassed", "gave up".

**4. What they think causes it (the assumed cause)**
What they currently blame. Often wrong, or only half right.
Used for: "not your fault" and "real cause" angles. This is one of the most valuable fields. If you find it, it often becomes the best angle.

**5. The real cause, if the research or the brand shows one**
What actually drives the problem, from the brand's real information or well known general knowledge.
Used for: myth-breaker and real-cause angles. Never invent a cause.

**6. What they already tried, and why it failed**
Products, routines, hacks, professionals. Get the reason each one failed, in their words.
Used for: "if you've tried X" angles, comparison sections, the "it's not you" move.

**7. What they want instead**
The outcome described the way they'd describe it to a friend. Small, concrete wins beat big transformations ("my makeup goes on smooth" beats "confidence").
Used for: the promise in headlines, the after-state.

**8. What scares them about buying (objections)**
Sort each one into a type:
- Price: "why not just buy the cheap one?"
- Evidence: "how do I know it works?"
- Fit: "it might work for others, not me"
- Risk: "what if it makes it worse / I waste the money?"
- Trust: "why believe this brand?"
Used for: fear-killer angles, the subheadline, the FAQ. The top objection must be answered near the top of the page.

**9. Words they use**
Exact phrases that come up again and again. Note the ones that are vivid ("sandpaper", "peach fuzz").
Used for: everywhere. These are the phrases to put in the copy.

**10. Words they'd never use**
Category or marketing language that would make them feel sold to ("vellus hair", "exfoliation technology", "radiant complexion").
Used for: a list of words to keep out.

**11. Who they are, only where the quotes show it**
Age range, life stage, situation, what was happening when they bought. Only what the research shows, with counts ("7 of 12 quotes mention being over 45"). Gender too: if the quotes clearly come from women, the avatar is a woman ("she/her"); from men, a man ("he/him"). Give the evidence ("11 of 14 quotes are from women"). If it's mixed or unclear, write "they" and flag it as a gap. Never pick a gender because the category "usually" skews one way.
Used for: call-out angles and headlines that flag the reader ("over 50", "new runners", "dog owners with a senior dog").

**12. Awareness stage**
How much they already know (Eugene Schwartz's five stages):
- Unaware: doesn't know there's a problem worth solving. Rare in paid traffic.
- Problem-aware: knows the problem, doesn't know the kind of product that solves it.
- Solution-aware: knows the kind of product, is comparing options. Most DTC buyers.
- Product-aware: knows this product, isn't sure yet.
- Most aware: ready, needs a reason to act now.
Used for: deciding what the headline leads with. Problem-aware: lead with the problem. Solution-aware: lead with what's different. Product-aware: lead with proof. Most aware: lead with the offer.

Signals: quotes asking "what should I use for X?" mean problem-aware. Quotes comparing two products or kinds of product mean solution-aware. Reviews of this product mean product-aware.

## Also note

- **Triggers:** what made them finally look for a fix (an event, a photo, a comment from someone).
- **Who influences them:** dermatologist, vet, coach, partner, a creator they follow. Only from the research.
- **Contradictions:** if quotes disagree (some love the result, some hate the regrowth), keep both. Contradictions are where the sharp angles hide.

## Weak avatar warning signs

Flag these to the user instead of working around them:
- Demographics with no problem ("women 35 to 55, income $75K+"). You can't write a headline for a demographic.
- Points with no quotes under them.
- Quotes that read like summaries ("users find it effective"). People don't talk like that.
- Only 5-star reviews. You need the 1 to 3 stars and the Reddit complaints to find fears and failed solutions.
