# Using the customer's words

The best copy often already exists, in a review or a Reddit comment. Your job is to find it, then decide whether to use it exactly, tighten it or rephrase it.

## Use it exactly when

- **It's vivid.** A word or image the reader can feel: "sandpaper", "peach fuzz", "crash", "itchy", "like a zombie". Keep it exactly as written.
- **It names the problem better than the category does.** "The way the foundation sits on my peach fuzz" beats any marketing version.
- **It repeats.** If several people use the same phrase, it's how the market talks. Use it.
- **It's a fear or objection in their voice.** "I'm scared it'll grow back thicker." Quote it, then answer it.
- **You're showing it as a quote.** A review line in quote marks, a testimonial, a "real customer" callout. Then it must be word for word, from a real person, and you must be able to point to where it came from. Trim it if needed. Never add to it or change it.

## Tighten it when

- It's right but long. Keep their key words, cut the rest.
  "Honestly the way the foundation just sits on top of all my peach fuzz looks so bad to me" becomes "When foundation sits on your peach fuzz".
- It's in first person and you need second person for a headline. "My face felt like sandpaper" becomes "When your face feels like sandpaper". Their image, your sentence.

Tightened lines are not quotes. Don't put them in quote marks or present them as something a customer said.

## Rephrase it when

- **It's crude, a slur, or too personal** for an ad.
- **It names a competitor brand** in a way you can't use. Name the kind of product instead ("drugstore razors").
- **It makes a claim you can't make.** A customer can say "it cured my acne". The brand can't. Rephrase to what the brand can stand behind, or leave it out.
- **It's unclear without context.** If a reader wouldn't understand it alone, rewrite it in their style, not in marketing style.
- **It's a summary, not speech.** "Users find it effective" isn't how anyone talks. Go back to the research for a real line.

When you rephrase, stay close: their vocabulary, their level of formality, their feeling. Check it against the "words they'd never use" list.

## Never

- Make up a quote, a review, a testimonial or a customer name.
- Put words in quote marks that someone didn't say.
- Combine two real quotes into one fake one.
- Turn a customer's personal health claim into a brand claim.

## Where their words go

| Place | How |
|---|---|
| Headline | Their key phrase, tightened. Rarely a full quote (unless it's a proof-first ad headline). |
| Subheadline | Their fear or desired outcome, in their words. |
| First paragraph | At least one of their phrases, so the reader feels recognized straight away. |
| Ad hook | Their most vivid line, said out loud or on screen. |
| Proof sections | Full quotes, exact, with attribution. |
| FAQ | Their objections as the questions, in their words. |

## Quick test

Read your line and ask: would the person in the avatar say this to a friend? If not, it's marketing. Go back to the quotes.
