# Writing a page

Generation spends a credit and takes a couple of minutes, so the inputs matter more than anything you can do afterwards. This is how to decide them. The tool states what each input accepts and what the current options are; read it, then use the judgment below to fill them.

## Never ask what the conversation already answered

Work through the inputs in three passes:

1. **Use** what the user already said. Everything in this conversation about who the page is for, what problem it solves, what makes the product different, what they liked or hated about a previous page, belongs in the inputs, in their words. Do not make them repeat themselves.
2. **Infer** what follows clearly. A problem-first pitch means a problem-led listicle. A named reader ("guys over 40") folds into the angle rather than becoming another question. Unmentioned length takes the default.
3. **Ask** only what genuinely changes the page and cannot be read from context. Usually two things: format and look. Ask both in one plain-language message, and only for what is still open. If they already said both, ask nothing and go.

Then tell them it is starting and roughly how long it takes, so the wait reads as expected rather than as a hang.

## Angle versus context

This distinction decides the page, and getting it backwards is the most common way to waste a generation.

**Angle is a hard framing constraint.** Who the page is for and what story it tells. Every headline and every section has to serve it. Keep it to reader and story.

**Context is material the writer draws on where it fits.** Facts about the product, offer or situation that the brand's own site does not say: how it is actually made, what customers keep complaining about, a competitor claim to answer, guarantee or shipping details, results the founder has seen.

Putting facts in the angle bends the entire page toward being about those facts. Putting the reader in the context means nothing enforces it. An avatar digest goes in context, and the reader it describes goes in angle.

## Format

**Advertorial** is story-driven long-form. Use it when the product needs a mechanism explained or a belief changed before the offer makes sense, and when the reader does not yet know they have the problem.

**Listicle** is an "N reasons" list. Use it when the case is cumulative, when the reader is comparison shopping, or when there is no single dramatic story to tell.

Listicles have a length and an opening approach. Infer both rather than asking: "keep it tight" is a length instruction, and how the user pitched the page tells you whether it should open on the problem or on the product.

## Template

The premium templates are not skins over the same page. Each one replaces structure rather than decorating it, so choosing one changes which sections exist and what the page is arguing. Read the current options in the tool description and match the template to the job:

- Is the page presenting a specific deal, or making a case?
- Is the blocking objection *"I do not believe this works"* or *"what if it does not work for me"*? Those want different templates.
- Is it a listicle? Then a listicle-specific premium look is available and a non-listicle premium template is not the right pairing.

If the user has not chosen a look, offer them the options rather than silently defaulting. Premium templates depend on the account's plan, and the tool refuses before spending anything if the account cannot use one, so offering the choice costs nothing and defaulting silently costs them the page they wanted.

## Voice

The default is a third-person publication voice. It is what makes an advertorial read like an article, and it is usually right when the page needs to look like journalism.

Switch to first person when the user says "in my voice", "as the founder", "from me", or when they have been telling their own story in the conversation. When they have named themselves, pass the name and role too: the name becomes the byline, and the role bounds what the page may claim on their behalf. If they have described how they talk anywhere in the conversation, pass that rather than asking again.

Founder voice is the stronger choice when the founder has an actual story to tell, and the weaker one when the page's credibility comes from reading as reporting.

## Brand

Prefer a saved brand over a URL. Re-analyzing a URL you already have a brand for wastes time and can leave the account with two profiles for one site. Look at the saved brands first, and analyze only when the brand is genuinely new.

If a saved brand exists but its facts are wrong or stale, say so and let the user decide, rather than quietly analyzing again.

## After generating

The page comes back as a draft as soon as the copy is written; images finish afterwards. Follow what the status tool tells you rather than polling on a fixed rule of your own, since a page can legitimately end up with images that no generation job produced. When it is settled, show the user the page and give them the link.
